Our research begins with businesses, industries, events or ideas worth understanding. Some leads come from news feeds; others come from filings, reports, watchlists or conversations.

The workflow below describes **what happens to a research lead**, including where we verify evidence, revisit earlier work and make editorial decisions. It is a guide to our practice, not a claim that every item advances to publication.

## 1. Ideas and intake

Collect news, filings, reports, industry studies and original research ideas. Tools help organize and flag promising leads.

## 2. Triage

Choose what deserves attention. Park or discard low-value leads rather than researching every headline.

## 3. Source collection

Gather primary documents and useful context. Collected references are **not** verified facts.

## 4. Verification

Check sources, reconcile figures and examine missing or conflicting information. Return to collection when the evidence is weak.

## 5. Analysis

Study the business, industry, accounts, valuation assumptions, risks and opposing case. Keep assumptions distinct from reported facts.

## 6. Drafting

Prepare the article, supporting charts or diagrams, and optional X copy. AI assists; it does not certify claims.

## 7. Editorial review

Challenge the reasoning and improve the writing. The owner can approve the draft, request revisions or hold it.

## 8. Publication approval

A **separate owner decision** authorizes an exact public article or post. A private research PR merge is not publication permission.

## 9. Publishing and learning

Release approved work, correct mistakes and revisit conclusions as evidence changes.

**Collected ≠ verified. Drafted ≠ published. Approval is human.**
